A useful reference can reveal what is not immediately visible on a stamp or letter. Philatelic literature includes catalogues, handbooks, studies, journals and documents that help identify items, understand their context and record discoveries. This initial selection suggests a route: start with a specific question and choose the source best suited to answering it.

## What kind of publication do you need?

A **catalogue** helps identify and arrange issues. Read its introduction and key first: abbreviations, numbers and criteria differ between publications. When recording a reference, include the catalogue title and edition as well as the stamp number. A catalogue valuation is not a promised buying or selling price.

A **specialist study** examines a defined subject, such as an issue, a postal marking or a route. A **journal or bulletin** follows new observations and discussions. **Exhibition and auction catalogues** introduce items and descriptions, but uncertain identifications should be checked against other sources.

## First readings and research tools

### Begin without buying a book

- [CTT — Organizar uma coleção](https://www.ctt.pt/particulares/filatelia/organizar-uma-colecao). Portuguese guidance on organising a collection. A useful starting point if you have yet to choose a direction.
- [American Philatelic Society — Getting Started](https://stamps.org/learn/getting-started). English introductions to first steps, equipment and identification.

### Identify and add context

- [Mundifil catalogue — Portugal, Azores and Madeira, 2024 edition](https://montrafilatelica.com/index.php?controller=product&id_product=309&id_product_attribute=0&rewrite=catalogo-portugal-mundifil-ano-2020). A concrete example of a catalogue for Portuguese collecting. This is a retailer's page identifying that edition; check the coverage you need and available editions before buying.
- [CTT — publishing programme](https://www.ctt.pt/particulares/filatelia/plano-editorial/). Books on subjects connected with stamp issues and Portuguese culture. Includes the _Portugal em Selos_ series, useful for exploring the subjects of each year's issues; it does not replace a specialist catalogue.

### Find studies and bibliographies

- [Clube Nacional de Filatelia — Filatelicamente, in the University of Porto web archive](https://www.up.pt/arquivoweb/www.fep.up.pt/docentes/cpimenta/lazer/WebFilatelicamente/public_html/index.html). An archived publishing portal with references to journals, catalogues and books, mainly in Portuguese. This is a historical resource; some older links may no longer work.
- [American Philatelic Research Library](https://stamps.org/services/library). A specialist library with a searchable catalogue. Useful for finding titles and authors; a catalogue record does not necessarily mean the full text is freely available.

## Keep a reference notebook

Choose an item and ask a question: “which issue is this?” or “what does this postmark mean?”. Record the author, title, edition or date, page and, for a digital source, its URL and your access date. Distinguish what a source establishes from the hypotheses you are still testing.

This page will grow with further annotated readings. To suggest a publication, send its full reference and a short explanation of its usefulness to collectors through the [NFB contact page](/en/contact/).
