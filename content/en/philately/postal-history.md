A letter preserves more than its message. Its envelope, postage and postal markings help explain how the mail worked. Postal history studies this evidence: routes, services and the charges paid, including correspondence from before adhesive postage stamps were introduced.

## Read both sides

Start by identifying the place of origin, destination and legible dates. Look for transit or arrival marks, registration labels and forwarding annotations. Not every letter carries all these details, and a single mark may not establish its whole journey. The [National Postal Museum explains what covers and letters can reveal](https://postalmuseum.si.edu/exhibition/about-philately/covers-and-letters).

**Franking** is the means of paying postage, for example with stamps. The **postal rate** is the charge applicable to the mailing. To establish whether a letter was correctly franked, you need the rates and conditions in force at the time, including destination, weight and additional services. Adding up the stamps' face values is only a beginning.

## A first exercise

Choose a letter or postcard connected with Braga or another place you know. Photograph both sides and prepare a record with three fields: “what I can read”, “what I have checked against a source” and “what remains uncertain”. This helps prevent a hypothesis from quietly becoming a fact.

Keep associated material with the letter. Cutting out a stamp or erasing an annotation can destroy evidence. For basic care, consult the [National Postal Museum's guidance on preserving letters](https://postalmuseum.si.edu/sites/default/files/preserving-your-letters1.pdf) (PDF).
