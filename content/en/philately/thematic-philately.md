Do you enjoy gardens, music, astronomy or transport? A personal interest can guide a stamp collection. Gathering items about a subject is a good beginning; thematic philately takes the next step by using them to develop a story, with a sequence and a central idea.

## From subject to plan

Instead of trying to collect “every stamp showing flowers”, imagine a small collection called “The life of a garden”. It could begin with seeds, move on to pollination and finish with caring for the space. This is simply an exercise: your plan should follow your questions and the material you discover.

Each item should contribute to the story. A short caption explains its role rather than merely repeating what the picture shows. The [FIP Thematic Philately guidelines](https://www.f-i-p.ch/wp-content/uploads/TH_GUIDELINES_2018_New_FINAL.pdf) explain how a theme, a plan and its development fit together (PDF).

## More than stamp pictures

A thematic collection can include stamps, postal markings and other postal material. A cancellation referring to an activity may serve the story as well as a stamp's design. Competitive exhibiting has specific selection criteria: a privately printed illustration on an envelope does not automatically have the same philatelic significance as an official postal issue.

As a first exercise, arrange six items on a temporary page. Write a connecting sentence between each pair. If an item does not move the story forward, keep it for another section or reconsider your plan. You can collect for the pleasure of discovery without having to enter exhibitions.

## Develop your idea together

By [joining the NFB](/en/about/membership/), you can share your idea, exchange perspectives and learn more with fellow members. A subject you already know outside philately may offer a new way of looking at it.
