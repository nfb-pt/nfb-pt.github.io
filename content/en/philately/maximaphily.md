Maximaphily involves studying and collecting **maximum cards**: items in which a picture postcard, a stamp affixed to its picture side and a postal cancellation form a coherent relationship. A cancellation is the postal marking that indicates the stamp has been used.

## Three elements in concordance

The key is **concordance**, the connection between the three elements. The postcard picture and the stamp's subject should correspond; the place and date of cancellation must also be appropriate to the subject and postal use. Matching colours alone is not enough. The [FIP maximaphily regulations](https://www.f-i-p.ch/wp-content/uploads/Maximaphily.pdf) explain the criteria in detail (PDF).

As an exercise, imagine a postcard and a stamp showing the same monument. Consider which cancellation location would have a justified connection with the building and which dates would be admissible. This example helps you ask questions; it does not establish that an item meets every exhibiting criterion.

## Look before you create

Start by examining existing maximum cards. Record the postcard subject, stamp issue and cancellation details separately. Note why you think they work together. If you want to make a card, first check the rules and available cancellation services with the postal operator; do not draw or imitate a postal marking.

Maximaphily also invites a closer look at photography, heritage and visual composition. The quality of the relationship between the elements matters more than collecting items at random.
