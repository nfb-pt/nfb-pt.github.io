A collection begins with a question: what sparks your curiosity? It might be a town, an animal, a period or letters received by your family. Philately involves collecting and studying stamps and other evidence of the postal service. You do not need a complete collection or expensive items to make discoveries.

## Choose a starting point

Begin with a small group you can examine closely. You could collect a country, a period or a theme. Mint and used stamps offer different clues: on used stamps, a postal marking may add a date and a place. [CTT's guide to organising a collection](https://www.ctt.pt/particulares/filatelia/organizar-uma-colecao) introduces possible approaches, in Portuguese.

Try choosing ten items and writing one sentence about each. Record the issuing country or authority, the subject, the face value — the value printed on the stamp — and what you would still like to discover. Do not confuse face value with an item's market price.

## Examine and protect

A magnifying glass, smooth-tipped stamp tongs and a stockbook are useful starting tools. Avoid glue, adhesive tape and albums with sticky pages. Keep your collection away from direct sunlight and damp. The [American Philatelic Society explains these tools](https://stamps.org/learn/getting-started/tools-needed-to-get-started) with examples.

Before removing a stamp from a letter, examine the whole item. The envelope, postmarks and annotations may be of interest for [postal history](/en/philately/postal-history/). If you are unsure how to separate something, leave it intact and ask for help: not every stamp or paper can withstand water or other treatments.

## Learn with others

By [joining the NFB](/en/about/membership/), you can keep exploring philately and learning with fellow members. Bring your questions and share what you have noticed; you do not need advanced knowledge to start.
