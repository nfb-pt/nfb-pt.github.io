A small image can suggest a long journey. A building invites questions about a place; a plant can spark curiosity about a landscape; an inscription may lead to unexpected reading.

### Look before classifying

It helps to begin with what we can describe. Which elements stand out? How are they arranged? Are there words or markings we cannot read with confidence?

Separating observations from hypotheses makes notes more useful. A well-recorded question can be revisited with a catalogue, a reference or another collector’s help.

### Put the image in context

Interest in a collection can grow when we look for relationships between items. A definitive connection is not always needed: comparing two depictions of the same subject already offers material for conversation.

A short caption can explain what caught our attention, record sources and identify what remains uncertain. Reading those notes later may reveal how our own perspective has changed.
