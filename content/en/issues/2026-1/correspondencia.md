A letter can invite us to look beyond the stamp. The envelope, the arrangement of the writing and the visible markings are all part of the object before us.

### Record without guessing

At first, it can be useful to describe the readable elements and mark uncertainties. A clear photograph allows us to return to a detail and share it with someone who may help with research.

When information is uncertain, a note such as “reading to be confirmed” preserves that distinction. A collection becomes clearer when questions are not mistaken for conclusions.

### The memory of objects

Keeping a reading reference or an item’s known origin helps reconstruct the research journey. That small record may become as interesting as the answer itself.

The starting point remains simple: look at the whole, notice the details and leave room to discover more.
