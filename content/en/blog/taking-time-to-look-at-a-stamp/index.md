A stamp takes up little space, yet it can raise many questions. Before identifying it or looking for a reference, we can spend some time considering what the image itself shows us.

## Images and words

What attracts attention first? It might be a figure, a landscape, a colour or a word. Looking at the relationship between these elements helps us describe the piece more clearly.

The inscriptions deserve the same attention. It is useful to note what we can read and mark any unclear word or number. A recorded question is more helpful than a hurried reading presented as a certainty.

## Small details matter

The outline, composition and visible markings can suggest further questions. This first look is not about reaching technical conclusions; it is a chance to learn how to describe what is present.

A photograph or a note can help when comparing two pieces later. The image should make it possible to distinguish the details we want to discuss.

## Share what you see

Showing the same piece to someone else may reveal an element we missed. In a conversation between collectors, simple questions can open paths for reading and research.

Taking time to look does not resolve every uncertainty. It does offer a more attentive starting point for further discovery.
