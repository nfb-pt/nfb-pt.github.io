There are many ways to begin looking at stamps. One is to choose a subject that already interests us: gardens, architecture, music or travel, for example. A theme offers a starting point without having to define the collection forever.

## A subject close to home

Before looking for new material, it can be worth examining the pieces we already have. Do particular images or subjects recur? Does a detail raise a question? The answer might suggest a small initial group.

A very broad theme can make it difficult to decide what to include. Instead of trying to gather everything at once, we can choose a more focused question and explore the pieces that help us consider it.

## A collection can tell a story

Arranging stamps by subject invites connections between images. Two pieces might offer different perspectives on the same place, or suggest an unexpected link between themes.

At this stage, a title and a short note for each group help explain the choices. This need not be a formal exhibition: the aim is to find a sequence that makes sense to the collector.

## Leave room for change

An interest can grow, branch out or change over time. Keeping a few questions open allows the collection to follow that process of discovery. Curiosity can be a useful guide to the next step.
